Lucky-number pricing & branding for Chinese buyers

In Chinese, numbers sound like words. A price, a phone number or a model number can quietly say "prosper", or "death". Here's how to make numbers work for you.

By 79897 Editorial · Updated October 2026 · 7 min read

Why it works: homophones

Mandarin has many words that sound alike, so digits pick up the meaning of the words they resemble. 8 (bā) echoes 发 (fā), as in 发财, "to get rich". 9 (jiǔ) echoes 久, "long-lasting". 6 (liù) echoes 流, "smooth". 4 (sì) sounds close to 死 (sǐ), "death", which is why many buildings skip the 4th, 14th and 24th floors.

The evidence that it moves money

  • Hong Kong's government auctions personalised plates. The single letter "R" fetched HK$25.5 million in 2023, and plates heavy with 8s draw strong bids. Plate auctions have raised about HK$600 million since 2006.
  • Sichuan Airlines paid CN¥2.33 million for +86 28 8888 8888.
  • Prices ending in 8 are common in Chinese retail where Western retail would use 9. Red envelopes favour 88, 168 and 888.

A five-step pricing play

  1. List every customer-facing number: prices, bundle sizes, SKUs, hotline, address, launch date.
  2. Run each through the number decoder. Remove any 4s and negative combinations.
  3. Re-price with the lucky price generator. Stay within ±5–10% of your target.
  4. A/B test 8-ending prices with Chinese-speaking audiences: mainland China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore, Malaysia and the diaspora.
  5. Pick launch and opening dates that avoid ghost month, and ideally fall on 8th, 18th or 28th days.
Keep it tasteful. Overdoing numerology can look gimmicky to younger urban shoppers. Treat lucky numbers as a small edge, not a substitute for product quality.
