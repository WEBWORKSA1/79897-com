China sourcing agent fees explained

A good agent usually saves more than they cost, through better unit prices, fewer defects and no wasted trips. Here's how pricing works and what's fair.

By 79897 Editorial · Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

The three pricing models

ModelTypical range*Good forWatch out for
Commission5–10% of purchase-order value (lower for large orders)Ongoing orders, full-service managementAn incentive to favour higher prices. Ask for factory invoices.
Flat fee per project/productAbout US$300 per product sourced. Supplier audits around US$100+.One-off sourcing and verificationScope creep, so define the deliverables
Monthly retainerAbout US$500–1,000+ per monthMultiple SKUs and continuous developmentMinimum terms and unclear hours

*Ranges drawn from published agent price lists and industry fee guides (2025–2026). Your quote will vary with product complexity and order size.

What a full-service agent should include

  • Supplier search and a shortlist of 3–5 verified factories
  • Price and MOQ negotiation
  • Sample collection and consolidation
  • Production follow-up and photo/video updates
  • Quality inspection (sometimes charged per person-day)
  • Shipping coordination and documents

Hidden costs and red flags

  • Hidden kickbacks from factories. Insist on transparent pricing and direct factory contracts where possible.
  • "Free" sourcing almost always means a mark-up on the unit price.
  • Payment to personal accounts, or bank details that change mid-deal.
  • No written scope for inspections, sample handling or re-work.

Questions to ask before you hire

  1. Are you paid by me only, or also by factories?
  2. Can I see the factory's original quotation and invoice?
  3. Who does the inspection, and to what AQL standard?
  4. What happens if goods fail inspection?
  5. Can you share two client references in my category?
